Adding one grain to a sandpile can leave it stable or trigger an avalanche across many sites. We use the two-dimensional Bak--Tang--Wiesenfeld model to examine how these responses depend on input location, dissipative boundaries and event grouping. The primary simulations comprised \R{productionGrains} measured grain additions across the baseline and three extensions, with five runs per condition. Four-grain batches used the same inputs as their sequential controls. At $L=64$, the uniform open model had mean height \R{base64height} and mean topplings \R{base64S_grain} per grain, compared with the finite-grid prediction \R{base64prediction}. Central input bias and cylindrical boundaries increased mean topplings to \R{bias64S_grain} and \R{cyl64S_grain}, while mean loss remained close to one per grain. Separate, longer confirmation runs gave height differences between biased and uniform input compatible with zero. All \R{matchedBatches} matched batches reproduced grouped sequential topplings and loss exactly. Their larger event sizes reflect the grouping of successive responses. Discrete fits favour power laws with an exponential cutoff over pure power laws on the finite ranges examined.
