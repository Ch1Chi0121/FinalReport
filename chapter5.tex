\subsection{Burn-in diagnostics}\label{sec:stationarity}
The pilot trajectories show initial mass accumulation followed by fluctuations around similar mean heights (Figure~\ref{fig:pilot}). Empty starts give a lower mean in the first $10L^2$-grain window. The longest estimated height or frequency correlation time was about 1,195 grains, making the chosen block about seven times longer. These diagnostics guided burn-in and blocking. Each starting state had one independent pilot chain.

\section{Correlations and the role of the source position}\label{sec:correlations}
We expected a positive correlation between $S$ and $A$: every site counted in the area must topple at least once, and some sites topple repeatedly. Larger areas should also tend to have greater spatial extent. Loss should depend on these quantities and on the input site's distance to a dissipative boundary. For a single-grain input at $(i_t,j_t)$ on the open square, we use
\[
 b_t=\min(i_t,j_t,L-1-i_t,L-1-j_t).
\]
This is the distance to the nearest dissipative boundary site, with $b_t=0$ on the boundary. Figure~\ref{fig:correlations} compares loss probabilities within fixed size bands and $b$ groups. All six Spearman correlations use $S>0$ and retain zero-loss avalanches.

At $L=64$, $\rho(S,A)=\R{rhoSA}$ with interval $\R{rhoSACI}$, $\rho(S,\ell)=\R{rhoSell}$ with $\R{rhoSellCI}$, and $\rho(A,\ell)=\R{rhoAell}$ with $\R{rhoAellCI}$. The differences between these observables reflect repeated topplings and variations in cluster shape. Loss is less strongly correlated: $\rho(S,D)=\R{rhoSD}$ with $\R{rhoSDCI}$, $\rho(A,D)=\R{rhoAD}$ with $\R{rhoADCI}$, and $\rho(D,\ell)=\R{rhoDell}$ with $\R{rhoDellCI}$.

\subsection{Full-support spatial bias}\label{sec:bias}
On the open square, we bias the input towards the centre using
\begin{equation}
 q_\eta(x)=\frac{1-\eta}{L^2}+\eta\,
 \frac{\exp[-\|x-c\|_2^2/(2\sigma^2)]}{\sum_y\exp[-\|y-c\|_2^2/(2\sigma^2)]},
 \quad \eta=0.9,\quad\sigma=L/8,\quad c=((L-1)/2,(L-1)/2).
\end{equation}
Every site has positive input probability, bounded below by $0.1/L^2$, so the recurrent stationary measure and its height statistics are preserved. The stationary mean response $\E[S]=q^\top w$ weights sites by their input probabilities. Bias towards central sites, farther from the sinks, therefore predicts more topplings per grain.

At $L=64$, mean topplings rise from \R{base64S_grain} to \R{bias64S_grain}, close to the benchmark of \R{bias64prediction} for the specified input distribution (Figure~\ref{fig:driving}). Dissipation probability falls from \R{base64p_loss} to \R{bias64p_loss}, while mean loss conditional on dissipation rises from \R{base64D_positive} to \R{bias64D_positive}. Mean loss per grain is \R{bias64D_grain}. The triggering probability can also change: biased input samples sites with different spatial weights even when the stationary law of the grid stays the same.

\subsection{Cylindrical boundaries}\label{sec:cylinder}
For the cylinder, columns are periodic and grains leave only above the top row or below the bottom row. At the simulated widths $L\geq3$, a toppling on a dissipative row loses one grain, including at the periodic seam. The graph distance is
\begin{equation}
 d_{\rm cyl}((i,j),(i_0,j_0))=|i-i_0|+\min(|j-j_0|,L-|j-j_0|),
\end{equation}
and the source distance to a dissipative boundary is $\min(i,L-1-i)$. Reach follows this cylindrical distance and is bounded by $L+\lfloor L/2\rfloor$.

A finite-grid benchmark for the stationary mean follows by taking $w$ constant along each row. Set $w_{-1}=w_L=0$ and
\begin{equation}
 w(i,j)=\frac{(i+1)(L-i)}{2}.
\end{equation}
The horizontal neighbours cancel two diagonal contributions, giving $(K_{\rm cyl}w)_{i,j}=2w_i-w_{i-1}-w_{i+1}=1$, including on the dissipative rows. Averaging over uniform input yields
\begin{equation}
 \E[S]_{\rm cyl}=\frac1L\sum_{i=0}^{L-1}\frac{(i+1)(L-i)}2
               =\frac{(L+1)(L+2)}{12}.\label{eq:cylinder}
\end{equation}
Substitution into the sparse matrix verifies this analytic result.

Bottom left: mean stable height with 95\% seed intervals. Bottom right: positive-loss ($D>0$) CCDFs at $L=64$. Each curve uses five primary seeds and one grain per event.}

With fewer dissipative edges, grains stay longer and generate more topplings. The boundary change affects both height statistics and response, while stationary loss remains one per input grain. At $L=64$, mean loss is \R{cyl64D_grain}, with dissipation probability \R{cyl64p_loss} and conditional mean loss \R{cyl64D_positive}.

\subsection{Batches as an exact grouping experiment}\label{sec:batch}
Each batch draws four independent uniform sites with replacement and adds their grains before relaxation. We compare this with sequential single-grain additions from the same stable $h$, using the same ordered sites $s_1,\ldots,s_m$. A toppling in the sequential schedule remains legal if the additional grains from later inputs are already present. Concatenating those schedules therefore stabilizes the batched input legally. Uniqueness of the odometer gives
\begin{equation}
 h_{\rm batch}=h_{\rm sequential},\quad
 u_{\rm batch}=\sum_{r=1}^m u_r,\quad
 S_{\rm batch}=\sum_rS_r,\quad D_{\rm batch}=\sum_rD_r.\label{eq:batch}
\end{equation}
Area counts the union of toppled sites, so the sum $\sum_r A_r$ counts shared sites more than once. The toppling and loss identities hold for each input sequence, including correlated consecutive avalanches.

If $P$ is the one-grain transition, independent sources give batch transition $P^m$. Every stationary measure of $P$ is stationary for $P^m$. The sampled chain $P^m$ may split into several recurrent classes or retain periodicity. Starting from an equilibrated baseline grid and matching the inputs isolates the effect of grouping. Each batch is fully relaxed before the next one arrives.

Across both sizes, all \R{matchedBatches} batches match the grouped controls exactly in $S$, $D$ and final mass. Grid configurations and height frequencies also agree whenever sampled. Independent small-grid tests verify the full odometer and area-union identities. At $L=64$, \R{overlap64}\% of batches have overlapping toppled sets, and summing sequential areas exceeds the mean union by \R{overcount64} sites per batch. These overlaps account for the difference between the two area curves in Figure~\ref{fig:batch}.
